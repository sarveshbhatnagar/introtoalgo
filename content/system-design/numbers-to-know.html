A candidate designs a checkout page with a 200 ms budget. To show a price it calls tax, stock and fraud services one after another, all in another region. At about 100 ms a round trip, the page takes over 300 ms before doing any work of its own. Nobody noticed until the interviewer asked.

You need about twenty numbers, each right within a factor of 3, and the habit of using them. All are rounded and vary with hardware, distance and load, so state them as assumptions.

The ratios matter more than the values. A same-region call is about 5,000 memory reads; a cross-region call is about 200 same-region calls.

Sizes and throughput

  • Small values: ASCII character 1 byte, integer or timestamp 8 bytes, UUID 16 bytes, metadata row 100 bytes to 1 KB.
  • Media: a photo a few hundred KB to a few MB. Video at 5 Mbit/s is 5 ÷ 8 × 60 ≈ 40 MB a minute.
  • Powers of two: 210 ≈ a thousand, 220 ≈ a million, 230 ≈ a billion. 32-bit IDs run out at 232 ≈ 4.3 billion, so use 64-bit IDs for anything that could pass a few billion rows. The count × size table to memorize is in estimation.
  • Throughput: memory about 10 GB/s, SSD about 1 GB/s, a 10 Gbit/s link about 1.25 GB/s. Use these to price a replica rebuild, a backup or a reshard.

Time and capacity

A day is 86,400 seconds, about 105. A month is about 2.6 million seconds, a year about 31.5 million. So 1 million events a day is about 12 a second; call it 10 and say so.

Single-server capacities vary tenfold with hardware and query shape, so state these as orders of magnitude:

  • A stateless API server: about 1,000 requests a second for typical work.
  • A relational database primary: a few thousand writes a second; reads by index into the tens of thousands.
  • An in-memory cache node: about 100,000 simple reads a second.
  • Open WebSocket connections on one server: tens of thousands, up to about 100,000 mostly idle.
  • One large machine: hundreds of GB of memory, a few TB of SSD. If your data fits, say so: one machine is the simplest design.

Parts that must all work multiply: three services at 99.9% each give 0.9993 ≈ 99.7%, about 2 hours down a month. Redundant copies, where any one is enough, multiply their downtime: two at 99% give 1 − 0.012 = 99.99%, if they fail independently. More in reliability and failure.

In the interview

When a decision rests on speed, size or uptime, bring in a number and finish with "so":

  • "A cross-region call is about 100 ms, so it can't be on the checkout path."
  • "50 million users at 1 KB each is 50 GB, so sessions fit in one cache node, plus a replica."

Expect this follow-up:

  • "Why can't cross-region be faster?" Light in fibre covers about 200 km per ms. New York to London is about 5,600 km, so a round trip takes at least 2 × 5,600 ÷ 200 = 56 ms.

Common mistakes

Three independent 100 ms calls made at once take about 100 ms, not 300. Say which calls depend on each other before adding.

"An SSD read is 87 µs" invites a debate you cannot win. Say "about 0.1 ms"; the order of magnitude carries the decision.
