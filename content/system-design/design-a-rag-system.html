An engineer asks the company assistant how to rotate the staging database password and gets four steps, each linked to its runbook page. Behind that, a system found 8 relevant passages among 50 million, checked the engineer may read each, and gave them to a language model told to answer only from them.

This is retrieval-augmented generation (see RAG basics and chunking and retrieval). The model call is easy. The hard parts: keeping millions of documents fresh in the index, finding the right passages fast, never leaking a document, and measuring answer quality. The steps follow A Framework for Any Design.

Clarify the problem

Design a system that answers employees' questions from company documents. Which documents: a wiki, tickets, shared drives? Can every employee read everything? All three, about 10 million documents. Permissions vary a lot: HR, legal and some engineering spaces are restricted. Then permission checks are a hard requirement. How many people, and how fresh must answers be after an edit? 20,000 employees. A changed page should show up within minutes. My priorities: never show content someone can't open, cite every answer, say "I don't know" rather than guess, stream first words within about 2 seconds, and control cost per question. Asking about permissions in the first minute marks someone who has built one of these, not just seen a demo.
  • Functional: cited answers from documents the asker may read; say when the documents lack the answer; ingest changes; collect feedback.
  • Non-functional: zero permission leaks; first words within 2 seconds, full answer within about 10; edits searchable within 10 minutes, removed access within 5; bounded cost per question; available in working hours in every region.
  • Out of scope: taking actions such as filing tickets, training or fine-tuning models, public customers.

Estimate the scale

  • Chunks: 10 million documents × about 5 passages = 50 million chunks.
  • Questions: 20,000 people × 10 a day = 200,000 a day. Over 8 working hours, 200,000 ÷ 28,800 s ≈ 7 a second; about 20 at a 3x peak. Few, but each is expensive.
  • Changes: 1% of documents changing daily is 100,000 documents, about 500,000 chunks to re-embed: about 6 a second.

Write the endpoints for the chat window and for connectors reporting changes. One response, or a stream?

An ask endpoint that streams the answer and ends with citations, a feedback endpoint, and an internal change-event endpoint for connectors.

The decision to argue is streaming. A full answer takes seconds, but the first words arrive in about one, so server-sent events meet the 2-second target. Citations come last because the model picks sources as it writes. And when the model is down, the 503 still carries useful ranked documents.

What must an indexed chunk hold to be searched, cited, permission-filtered and re-embedded only when needed?

Text, vector, document id, title and link for citations, allowed groups, and a text hash so unchanged chunks are not re-embedded.

Model the data

documents                         -- source of truth for what is indexed
  doc_id          VARCHAR(64) PRIMARY KEY     -- "wiki-4410"
  source          VARCHAR(16)
  title, url      TEXT
  allowed_groups  TEXT[]          -- copied from the source system
  content_hash    CHAR(64)
  updated_at      TIMESTAMP

chunks                            -- lives in the search index
  chunk_id        VARCHAR(80)     -- "wiki-4410#3"
  doc_id          VARCHAR(64)
  text            TEXT            -- about 500 tokens, about 2 KB
  embedding       VECTOR(1024)
  allowed_groups  TEXT[]          -- filter field
  text_hash       CHAR(64)        -- skip re-embedding unchanged chunks
  index_version   INT             -- which chunker and embedding model made it

The hot query is "nearest vectors and best keyword matches, among chunks this person may read". That needs a search index with vector search, keyword search and filters together: a search engine with vectors, or a vector database with keywords. At smaller scale, a relational database with a vector extension is fine. Vector search is approximate, trading some recall for speed, so measure recall.

Sketch both paths: document to searchable chunks, question to cited answer. Where is the permission check?

Ingestion: workers fetch, chunk, embed and upsert changed documents with their allowed groups. Query: embed, filtered hybrid search, rerank, top chunks to the model. Permissions are a filter inside the search.

High-level design

Both paths use the same embedding model, because vectors from different models cannot be compared.

Deep dives

The ingestion pipeline

Connectors take each source's change events and also crawl on a schedule to catch missed ones. Each change becomes a queue message for workers:

  1. Fetch and extract the text, including from PDFs and slides. Extraction errors are the most common silent failure; count them per source.
  2. Chunk along headings, about 500 tokens with a little overlap, with the title and section heading atop each chunk so it stands alone.
  3. Hash each chunk and embed, in batches, only those whose hash changed. Most edits touch a few chunks.
  4. Upsert and delete by doc_id: write new and changed chunks, remove ones that no longer exist.

A new embedding model or chunker means a new index: old and new vectors cannot be compared. Build it alongside with a new index_version, write live changes to both, and switch when complete. At an assumed 1,000 chunks a second, backfilling 50 million takes 50,000 seconds, about 14 hours.

Retrieval quality versus latency

Vector search finds the same meaning in different words; keyword search finds exact terms such as error codes, product names, "staging". Company questions are full of exact terms, so use both, combined with reciprocal rank fusion: each list gives a chunk 1 ÷ (60 + rank) points, and totals are sorted.

ChunkKeyword rankVector rankScore
Staging runbook, step list141/61 + 1/64 = 0.0320
Staging environment setup361/63 + 1/66 = 0.0310
Password policy overviewnot found11/61 = 0.0164

Chunks found by both rise to the top, even when neither ranked them first. Then a reranker, a model reading the question and one chunk together, scores the candidates more accurately, at one model pass each.

I would pick hybrid plus reranker: 200 ms is small next to a second of generation, and better chunks let you send 8 instead of 20.

Permissions

Restricted text must never reach the prompt. Filter during retrieval, using permissions copied from the source onto every chunk.

  • Filter inside the search, not after it. Dropping unreadable chunks from the top 50 can leave almost nothing: someone in a small team may find 48 of 50 hidden.
  • Sync permission changes fast and separately. An ACL change only updates allowed_groups, so it is cheap. Run it more often than content sync: removed access within 5 minutes.
  • Read the asker's groups fresh from the directory, cached for a minute, not from a login token that lives for hours. People lose access by leaving groups too.
  • Recheck sensitive sources at answer time. For HR or legal, ask the source system whether the user can still open each cited document, closing the gap before the sync.

Retrieved text is also untrusted: a page can contain text written to steer the model. Treat it as data (see prompt injection).

"Answer only from documents the user can access" in the prompt does nothing: the model cannot check permissions. Anything in the prompt can appear in the answer, so filter first.

Caching and cost

Cost is the 900 million tokens a day. The levers, in order:

  • Send fewer, better chunks. 6 chunks instead of 8 cuts context from 4,000 to 3,000 tokens per question.
  • Cache answers to repeated questions, keyed by the normalized question and the user's group set (without groups, answers leak across access levels). Evict an entry on any content or ACL event for a document it cites. Free-form questions repeat less than you hope; measure the hit rate.
  • Put the prompt's fixed part first. Some model servers reuse work for a repeated prompt prefix.
  • Route easy questions to a smaller, cheaper model, checked with the evaluation set below.

Be careful with a "semantic" cache that answers similar questions: "parental leave in France" and "parental leave in Germany" are similar and have different answers.

Evaluation

Without measurement, every change is a guess. Build a golden set of a few hundred real questions labelled with the documents that answer them, and score every change before shipping (see evals):

  • Retrieval recall at 8: how often is a right chunk among the 8 sent? If not, no prompt can save the answer.
  • Faithfulness: is every claim supported by a cited chunk? A model can grade this at scale; spot-check it against people.
  • Correct "I don't know": include unanswerable questions and score the refusals.

In production, track thumbs down, citation clicks, the "I don't know" rate and answers per source; add failures to the golden set.

Bottlenecks and failures

At 10x the questions, about 200 a second at peak, the model and reranker run out first: add capacity, cache more, cut tokens. At 10x the documents, 2 TB of vectors at 4 bytes per number, shard the index by chunk, query shards in parallel, and store numbers in 1 byte.

Follow-up questions

How do you handle follow-up questions in a conversation?

Rewrite it as a standalone question from recent turns ("And for production?" becomes "How do I rotate the production database password?"), then retrieve. The raw follow-up finds nothing.

The answer is wrong but the right document exists. How do you debug it?

Check retrieval first: was the right chunk in the 50 candidates, and the final 8? If not, fix chunking, search or ranking; if so, look at the prompt and model.

Why not put all the documents in the context window?

Ten million documents fit in no context window, big windows cost tokens on every question, and models use long contexts unevenly. Retrieval sends a few thousand relevant tokens.

How do you handle tables and spreadsheets?

Chunk by rows, repeating the header in each chunk. For numeric questions such as "sum this column", give the model a tool that queries the data.

How would you deploy this in several regions?

Replicate the index to each region and run answer services and models near users; ingestion stays central. If law keeps documents in a region, keep separate indexes and route by region.
