Reading ten case studies feels like progress. Then the clock starts, someone is watching, and you hear yourself say "um, so, a database" with no plan. Reading trains recognition; interviews test saying a design out loud, on time, while interrupted. So practise timed and spoken, on unseen prompts.

Run a 45-minute mock

Use the real interview's split, with a partner or alone.

Seven prompts

Run the whole mock before opening a box's notes.

A mobile game with 20 million daily players wants a global leaderboard and a "your rank" screen.

Ask: global or friends too? Daily, weekly or all-time? How often do scores change? Must rank 9,000,000 be exact, or is "top 45%" fine?

Deep dives: rank in O(log n) with a sorted set; 20 million entries at about 100 bytes is 2 GB, so one in-memory node plus a replica, and sharding is not the hard part; rejecting impossible scores.

Check yourself: I estimated peak score updates. I rebuilt the board from durable scores when its node died.

A chain of 500 garages wants online reservations and entry gates that keep working when the network is down.

Ask first: "The class design or the distributed system?" Then: specific spots or a count per type (compact, EV)? How long is a spot held for a late driver?

Deep dives: no double booking, via a conditional decrement on (garage, type, time slot); an offline gate with today's reservations cached locally and synced later; charging once even when the gate retries.

Check yourself: I said the scale is small. I made payment idempotent.

10,000 servers each send 200 metric values every 10 seconds. Engineers want charts for the last 30 days and alerts.

Ask: how fresh must charts be? How long is full resolution kept? How many label combinations? Does data arrive late?

Deep dives: ingest of 10,000 × 200 ÷ 10 = 200,000 points a second, buffered through a stream; compressed time-series storage partitioned by metric and time; 1-minute and 1-hour rollups, so a 30-day chart reads 720 hourly points per series, not 259,200 raw ones.

Check yourself: I planned retention. I said what happens to alerts if ingestion falls behind.

Users submit solutions to programming problems: 10,000 submissions an hour, 10 times that during contests.

Ask: which languages? Time and memory limits per test? A contest scoreboard? (Say yourself that the code is untrusted.)

Deep dives: a fresh sandbox per run, with no network and CPU and memory limits; workers sized for contests: 10,000 an hour is about 3 a second, 30 at peak, and at 10 seconds a run that is 30 × 10 = 300 runs at once; results by polling or push.

Check yourself: I treated submitted code as hostile. I said what users see when the queue is long.

Readers comment under articles on a news site. Most articles get a few comments; a breaking story gets thousands an hour.

Ask: how deep do replies nest? Newest or top first? Moderate before or after publishing? Must new comments appear live?

Deep dives: a parent id per comment plus a path for sorting, with cursor pagination; caching the breaking story's first page for seconds; async moderation that can hide a comment later.

Check yourself: I showed one page's query. I said what users see during moderation.

Show owners of short links clicks per hour, top countries and unique visitors. Peak traffic is 20,000 clicks a second.

Ask: fresh to the second or the hour? Exact unique visitors, or within 1%? How long are raw clicks kept? Do bots count?

Deep dives: stream aggregation into per-link hourly counters, keeping raw events for reprocessing; approximate uniques with a sketch such as HyperLogLog, under 1% error in about 12 KB per counter; duplicate and late events. It extends the URL shortener.

Check yourself: I kept analytics off the redirect path. I fixed a counting bug by replaying raw events.

Timed auctions. Most get a few bids, but the last minute of a popular one gets hundreds of bids a second.

Ask: minimum increments? Does a late bid extend the end? What do watchers see live? When is the winner charged?

Deep dives: ordering bids on one auction with a conditional write "only if higher than the current top", or one owner per auction; pushing new top bids to watchers over persistent connections; closing on the server's clock, never the client's.

Check yourself: I serialised bids per auction, not globally. I sized the live fan-out.

In the interview

Use the same opening on every prompt, so something automatic comes out while you think:

  • "So the scope is [restate it]. A few questions on features, then scale."
  • "I'll get one path working end to end, then go deep on [the hard part]."

Practise the closing questions too: "What would you build first?", "What is weakest?", "How would you know it works?"

Common mistakes

Silent practice skips the hard part: saying it clearly under time. Talk out loud and record.
