A candidate designing a photo-sharing app is told "10 million daily users". They spend four minutes dividing by 86,400, get 3,472.2 requests a second, and then draw the design they would have drawn for 30 or for 300,000. The interviewer learned nothing about their judgment.

Estimation has one job: find out which numbers are big enough to change the design. You want the right order of magnitude in under four minutes, out loud, and then a sentence that starts "so...".

The handful of facts you need

Almost every estimate is built from these conversions. Memorize them.

The powers of two drift from the powers of ten (240 is about 10% more than 1012), which never matters at this precision. Interviews use powers of ten: 1 KB = 1,000 bytes.

  • A day is about 100,000 seconds. The real figure is 86,400. Dividing by 100,000 gives rates about 14% low; say so once and move on.
  • A month is about 2.5 million seconds (30 × 86,400 ≈ 2.6 million). A year is about 30 million (365 × 86,400 ≈ 31.5 million).
  • 1 million a day is about 10 a second. 1 billion a month is about 400 a second.
  • Sizes: an ASCII character is 1 byte, an integer or a timestamp 8 bytes, a UUID 16 bytes. A small database row is a few hundred bytes. Photo sizes and server capacity are for the interviewer to give or for you to state as assumptions.

Latency figures (a memory read, a disk seek, a cross-region round trip) live in numbers to know.

From daily users to servers

The core chain: daily users times actions per user gives requests a day; divide by 100,000 for the average rate; multiply by a peak factor; divide by what one server handles. Step through it for the photo app.

Each number should end with "so": so it fits on one machine, so it needs a CDN, so we must partition.

Storage, bandwidth and memory

Storage is items a day × bytes per item × days kept × copies. Bandwidth is requests a second × bytes per response. Cache memory is the number of hot items × bytes per item. Try each before opening the answer.

Two traps on the way. Network links are quoted in bits a second and storage in bytes: multiply bytes by 8. And durable storage almost always keeps more than one copy, so state your replication factor or the storage figure is a third of the real one.

How precise to be

What would make you more careful: a number close to a real limit. If peak traffic comes out at 900 a second and your assumed single-server limit is 1,000, the rounding matters; say "that is close to one server's limit, so I'll plan for two or more".

In the interview

Estimate after clarifying and before the API, as in the framework. Ask for the inputs, state any you assume, and compute only what can change the design: usually peak rate, total storage, and bandwidth or cache size.

  • "I'll round a day to 100,000 seconds, so my rates are a bit low."
  • "That's 1 GB a second of images, so images go through a CDN and never touch the API servers."
  • "Storage is 1 PB a year, so photos go in blob storage and the database only holds metadata."
Assume 10 million daily users. How much traffic is that? About 30 requests each is 300 million a day. Over roughly 100,000 seconds that's 3,000 a second, and I'll assume 9,000 at peak. Around 13 API servers at 70% load. So the API tier is not the hard part. Then what is? The photos. 5,000 views a second at 200 KB is 1 GB a second out, and a petabyte a year in. The design is mostly about blob storage and the CDN. Every number leads to a decision, and the estimate tells the candidate where to spend the rest of the hour.

Follow-ups you should expect:

  • "Where does a peak factor of 3 come from?" It is an assumption about daily patterns. Say what would raise it: a single time zone, a live event, a launch.
  • "How sure are you?" "Within a factor of 2 or 3, which is enough to choose the architecture."

Common mistakes

Computing storage, bandwidth, memory and servers and then saying nothing about them wastes four minutes. Finish every estimate with the decision it drives, or skip it.

Average rates size nothing: systems fall over at peak. Storage without replication is a third of the disk you will buy. Apply both, out loud.
